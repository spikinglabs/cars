Data and sources

Where Allo Cars gets its numbers, what you can download, and how an AI assistant can use the tools.

Charging prices

Public ad-hoc charging prices

Every hour we read the published ad-hoc tariffs (pay without a contract) of public chargers in Germany from the Open ChargePoint Database run by MobiData BW. It merges the AFIR price feeds that operators publish on Mobilithek. We keep only sources with an open licence, and we keep every collection, so the archive grows by itself. No open archive of earlier prices exists, so our series starts on 6 Nov 2026.

Free to download, no key and no sign-up:

  • /data/charging-prices/latest.json: today's median, 10th and 90th percentile, overall and per source
  • /data/charging-prices/daily.csv: one row per day and source
  • /data/charging-prices/tariffs.csv: every current ad-hoc tariff with energy, time, parking and session prices
  • /data/charging-prices/operators.csv: median price per operator, overall and for AC, DC (below 150 kW) and high-power (150 kW and up) charge points
  • /data/charging-prices/daily_power.csv: one row per day and charger type (ac, dc, hpc), counted per charge point
  • /data/charging-prices/daily_operators.csv: one row per day and operator
  • /data/charging-prices/sources.json: sources and their licences

Prices are the base energy price of each ad-hoc tariff. Session and blocking fees are in tariffs.csv. Contract prices are usually lower. For prices by charger type and operator we join each tariff to the charge points it applies to (OCPDB tariff associations), read their power from the charge point list and their operator from the weekly locations list, and count every charge point once, with its cheapest ad-hoc tariff.

When you reuse the data, credit the sources and licences listed in sources.json.

Electricity prices

Day-ahead electricity prices

Several times each afternoon we read the day-ahead prices of the German power exchange (bidding zone DE-LU) from SMARD, the energy market portal of the Bundesnetzagentur. Since October 2025 they come in 15-minute steps; tomorrow's prices are published around 13:00. Our history goes back to October 2018.

The price you pay on a dynamic tariff is the exchange price plus a fixed add-on (grid fee, levies, electricity tax, supplier margin) plus 19 % VAT. We assume 0.18 €/kWh before VAT; enter yours on the page.

  • /data/electricity-prices/de/latest.json: today and tomorrow in 15-minute steps, with the cheapest 1 to 8 hour windows
  • /data/electricity-prices/de/daily.csv: one row per day since 2018, in €/MWh
  • /data/electricity-prices/de/hourly.csv: every hour since 2018, in €/MWh

Source: Bundesnetzagentur | SMARD.de, licence CC BY 4.0.

Recalls

Car recalls

Every day we read the weekly reports of the EU Safety Gate and keep every alert in the category Motor vehicles since 2019: cars, motorcycles, vans, trucks and e-scooters. Germany's Kraftfahrt-Bundesamt (KBA) reports each German vehicle recall there, as do the other EU countries, Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Northern Ireland. For the models in our tools that are also sold in the US, we add the recall campaigns of the US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA).

Free to download, no key and no sign-up:

  • /data/recalls/eu/latest.json: counts and the newest alerts
  • /data/recalls/eu/models.json and /data/recalls/eu/models/<model>.json: alerts per followed model, for example volvo-ex30
  • /data/recalls/eu/brands.json and /data/recalls/eu/brand/<brand>.json: alerts per brand
  • /data/recalls/eu/recalls.csv: every alert since 2019, one row each
  • /data/recalls/eu/stats.json: alerts per year and vehicle type, per component and per brand, with car alerts per 100,000 new cars registered in Germany (from our registrations data)
  • /data/recalls/us/models/<model>.json: NHTSA campaigns for the same models

Safety Gate data: © European Union, reused under Commission Decision 2011/833/EU. NHTSA data is a US government work in the public domain. Whether a particular car is affected depends on its VIN. Safety Gate has no component field: we sort each alert by the part its hazard text names first (brakes, airbags, high-voltage battery and so on), which is approximate.

Registrations

New cars by model

The Kraftfahrt-Bundesamt (KBA), Germany's federal motor transport authority, publishes how many passenger cars of each brand and model series were newly registered every month (statistic FZ10). We read each monthly table once it comes out, about a week after the month ends, and keep every month since December 2018.

Free to download, no key and no sign-up:

  • /data/registrations/de/latest.json: the newest month: all new cars by drive type, the top 20 models and the top 10 electric models, with the change on a year before
  • /data/registrations/de/models.json: every model ever listed, with its file, newest count, rank and last 12 months
  • /data/registrations/de/models/<slug>.json: one model per month, for example volvo-ex30.json
  • /data/registrations/de/monthly.csv: every model, every month (month, brand, model, slug, total, diesel, hybrid, phev, bev)
  • /data/registrations/de/market.csv: all new cars per month by drive type and the electric share
  • /data/registrations/de/brands.json: every brand per month, total and electric

Counts are new registrations in the month. hybrid includes plug-in hybrids, which have their own phev column since 2021. bev is battery electric only. Model names are as the KBA writes them; SONSTIGE means a brand's other models.

Source: © Kraftfahrt-Bundesamt, Flensburg, under the Data licence Germany, attribution, version 2.0. When you reuse the data, credit the KBA the same way.

Charger map

Public charge points

The charger map shows the Ladesäulenregister of the Bundesnetzagentur (Federal Network Agency), the official list of public charge points in Germany. It is published a few times a year; we check daily and rebuild the map when a new file appears. Only operators who completed the agency's notification are in it, so the real number of chargers is higher. Devices at the same place (within about 10 m) are shown as one location, with the highest power of any of its charge points. About 200 locations whose coordinates lie far from their own postcode are left out until the register fixes them.

Licence: CC BY 4.0, credit "Bundesnetzagentur.de". Free to download, no key and no sign-up:

  • /data/chargers/de/latest.json: counts overall, per federal state and per operator, the list of map cells, and the date of the register
  • /data/chargers/de/stations.csv: every location with position, power, charge points, plugs, operator, address and payment
  • /data/chargers/de/cells/<id>.json: the locations in one 0.5 × 0.5 degree square, id = floor(lat × 2)_floor(lon × 2), for example 101_13 around Cologne
  • /data/chargers/de/overview.json and places.json: counts on a 0.1 degree grid, and postcode and town centres
  • /data/chargers/de/stats.json: the statistics: charge points per federal state, district and town (50,000+ people) with population, battery electric cars, points per 100,000 people, electric cars per point and kW per electric car; charge points by power class, plug and year; top operators
  • /data/chargers/de/areas.json: power classes, plugs, years and top operators for every state, district and town, for comparing places
  • /data/chargers/de/districts.csv: the same per district as a table

Population: Statistisches Bundesamt (Destatis), Gemeindeverzeichnis, 31 December 2024. Battery electric cars: Kraftfahrt-Bundesamt, FZ1 vehicle stock by registration district, 1 January. Both under the Data licence Germany, attribution, version 2.0. The statistics count every device in the register, including the locations left off the map.

Map tiles © OpenStreetMap contributors, served by the OpenStreetMap Foundation. The map uses Leaflet (BSD-2-Clause), loaded from this site.

Vehicle catalog

Official consumption of every new car model

Every new car registered in the EU is reported with its type-approval (WLTP) figures. The European Environment Agency publishes them each year under Regulation (EU) 2019/631. Once a month we turn the newest year into one row per model and fuel type: official kWh per 100 km (electric energy at the plug, so charging losses are included), litres per 100 km, CO2, power and mass, averaged over the versions people actually bought, with how many were registered. Models no longer sold new stay in the list, back to 2021, so older cars are covered too: year is the year the figures come from (the newest year a model still sold well), years the first and last year it was sold new. The figures are official, not real-world: real driving uses more, most of all on the motorway and in winter.

Free to download, no key and no sign-up:

  • /data/vehicle-catalog/de/models.json and models.csv: every model with at least 20 new registrations in Germany in a year since 2021, petrol, diesel, plug-in hybrid and electric
  • /data/vehicle-catalog/de/electric.json: electric models only, compact
  • /data/vehicle-catalog/eu/models.json: the same for all of the EU-27

Licence CC BY 4.0: credit the European Environment Agency. The provisional year can still change until the EEA publishes the final data.

Ratings

Safety, environment and breakdown ratings

Model pages and the compare page show three independent ratings as headline facts, each linked to the full result at the source. Euro NCAP crash-tests new cars and gives 0 to 5 safety stars; the tests get tougher every few years, so compare stars of similar years. Green NCAP rates one tested version of a car from 0 to 5 stars for clean air, energy use and greenhouse gases. The ADAC Pannenstatistik counts the breakdowns the ADAC road patrol attended in a year per 1,000 registered cars, by model series and year of first registration. Once a week we read the star rating, the year and the breakdown rates, nothing else; the reports themselves stay with their publishers.

  • /data/ratings/de/ratings.json: euro_ncap.models, green_ncap.versions and adac_breakdowns, each with a link to the source

No open licence: the ratings belong to Euro NCAP, Green NCAP and the ADAC. Name them when you use the figures and link to their results.

Fuel prices

Petrol, diesel and LPG prices

Every week the European Commission publishes national average pump prices, including all taxes, in the Weekly Oil Bulletin (CC BY 4.0). We keep the full series since 2005 for every EU country and the EU average,. The chart is on the fuel price page.

  • /data/fuel-prices/weekly.csv: one row per week since 2005, € per litre
  • /data/fuel-prices/latest.json: this week, the change on last week, 4- and 52-week averages
  • Other countries: /data/fuel-prices/<country>/…, for example eu or at; the list is in index.json
Calculators

Car consumption figures

The EV charging cost tool starts each car with rough real-world consumption for four ways of driving: efficient, typical, motorway at 120 km/h, and winter. They are our estimates for comparison, not manufacturer figures. Every other electric car sold in Germany is in the list too, with the four levels estimated from its official figure (0.9, 1, 1.22 and 1.38 times WLTP, which matches our estimates on average). Change them to what your trip computer shows. The formula is open: kWh per 100 km × (1 + charging losses) × price per kWh. The code is in /ev-cost/calc.js.

For AI assistants

Use Allo Cars from an AI

  • Connector for Claude. Add https://cars.allo.land/mcp as a custom connector in Claude (Settings, Connectors, Add custom connector) or in any app that speaks MCP. Your assistant can then look up consumption, recalls, a used-car check, fuel, charging and electricity prices, registrations and chargers near a place itself, and link you to the page. It only reads public data and needs no account. We keep an anonymous record of each lookup (no IP address, no ID) to see which tools are used; see the privacy notice.
  • Copy for AI. Every tool has a button that copies your inputs, the results and the link as plain text you can paste into any assistant.
  • Links that carry the inputs. A tool's settings live in its address, for example /ev-cost/?car=Volvo EX30&p=0.35. An assistant can build such a link to open the tool already filled in.
  • Machine-readable lists. /tools.json describes each tool and its parameters, and /llms.txt lists the pages and data files.

Each page is also in German under /de/ and in Simplified Chinese under /zh/, with the same link parameters.

Privacy

What we keep about you

Nothing. There is no account, no cookie and no tracking. Your inputs stay in the page and its link. Your browser only stores your light or dark choice and whether you closed the install hint. Details are in the privacy notice.
